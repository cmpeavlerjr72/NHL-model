## Step 1: fold-1 selection (before any fold-2 fit)
- Grid b {1000, 3000, 10000, 30000} x w {0.5, 0.7, 0.85}. Fold-1 LL differences are tiny; the best LL cell is b 3000, w 0.5. In 2 SE of it: 6 of 12 cells (all three at b 1000, b 3000 w <= 0.7, b 10000 w 0.5). Cells outside it (b 3000 w 0.85, b 10000 w >= 0.7, b 30000) lose LL by 0.006-0.027 nats/game (z 2.2-4.2): too much shrinkage costs likelihood.
- Spread ratios do NOT move toward 1 with stronger shrinkage. The "against" ratio rises with b (1.11 at b 1000 w 0.5; 1.35 at b 10000 w 0.5; 1.79 at b 30000 w 0.85), and "for" is below 1 except in the over-shrunk corner. Mechanism: B1 refits the coefficient on the standardised rating, so shrinking the rating rescales the coefficient rather than damping the response; the realised spread depends on the fitted coefficient vs the holdout persistence, not on b directly.
- Minimum max|log ratio| among in-2SE cells: 0.1899 at (1000, 0.7); no other cell within 0.02. **Selected b 1000, w 0.7** (fold-1 for 0.827, against 1.177).

## Findings and proposals for the PM (not applied)
- The D27 rule cannot move the ratio: with the coefficient refit, b changes the rating's scale, not the response's persistence, so the fold-1 selection collapses to SB-R1's cell. A genuine fix must act on the response itself (for example, a shrunk or fixed-scale coefficient on the rating, an estimated persistence by pooling train seasons with a season-split check, or tying the coefficient to the fold-1 out-of-sample slope). Pre-registration needed; I did not run extra scope.
- Caveat for the pre-registered "spread over actual" gate: the actual "for" spread is about 1 pp with a very wide ratio CI, so the "for" gate is weakly informative; "against" is the informative one.
