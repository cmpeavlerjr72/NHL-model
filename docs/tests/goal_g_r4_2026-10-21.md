## 4. Why the guard fails for every arm: the ±3% band against sampling noise (`scripts/diag_goal_gr4_band_power.py`, labelled diagnostic)

- **Method.** Outcomes are redrawn as y* ~ Bernoulli(p) from the arm's own probabilities, so the arm is calibrated by construction. 2,000 draws.

| rows | SD of a bucket ratio | P(one bucket within ±3%) | P(all four within ±3%) | band passed with 95% probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fold-1 test 2023-24 (C1s-sa) | 0.032 | 0.62-0.67 | **0.20** | ±7.9% |
| fold-1 test 2023-24 (C1s) | 0.032 | 0.65-0.67 | 0.20 | ±7.9% |
| judged 2022-23 (fold 1 / fold 2) | 0.032 | 0.63-0.69 | 0.22 / 0.22 | ±8.0% / ±7.8% |
| judged 2023-24 (fold 2) | 0.031 | 0.64-0.68 | 0.21 | ±7.6% |

- **Consequence.** If the seasons were independent, an exactly right model would pass all three parity cells with probability about 0.011. It would pass the fold-1 guard and the fold-2 check together with probability about 0.04.
- **The ±3% band is about 1 SE of a one-season bucket ratio.** As a pass/fail on point estimates it cannot separate a right model from a wrong one at this sample size.
- **Under a calibrated-model ±8% band, C1s would also pass on fold 1** (1.068, 0.948). A per-season point check therefore cannot judge the INV-71 trailing / tied over-conversion on one season. The diagnosis found it in the closed loop and pooled over the full chain.
